GPS vs Geofencing for Attendance Tracking 2026

Comprehensive comparison of GPS tracking and geofencing technologies for employee attendance and time tracking systems.

  4. Best Use Cases

GPS Explained

Global Positioning System (GPS) tracking provides precise location data of an employee's device at the moment of clock-in or clock-out. This technology uses a network of satellites to determine exact latitude and longitude coordinates.

  • Precision: Typically 5-10 meters accuracy outdoors
  • Requirements: Smartphone with GPS enabled and location permissions granted
  • Power Impact: Moderate GPS usage consumes battery faster
  • Best For: Remote workers, field service teams, delivery drivers, distributed workforces

GPS tracking is ideal for businesses where employees work at multiple job sites throughout the day, or where the worksite location itself is the primary verification method.

Geofencing Explained

Geofencing creates a virtual boundary around a specific worksite location. When an employee's GPS coordinates cross into or out of this predefined boundary, the system triggers an action (such as allowing or denying a clock-in).

  • Boundary: Typically a circular area with 100-500 meter radius (configurable)
  • Trigger: System checks if GPS coordinates are inside the geofence at clock-in/out time
  • Configuration: Employers define the worksite coordinates and boundary size
  • Best For: Fixed worksites, construction sites, offices, warehouses, any business with a specific location

Geofencing is best suited for businesses with a primary worksite where employees must be physically present to clock in or out.

Key Differences

Precision & Accuracy

Feature GPS Geofencing
Typical Accuracy 5-10 meters Depends on GPS signal quality
Location Data Exact coordinates provided Yes/No: inside or outside boundary

Boundary Control

  • GPS: Reports exact location; employer decides if it's acceptable
  • Geofencing: System automatically accepts/rejects based on boundary
  • Combined: GPS provides data, geofence makes the decision
Use Cases

Best Use Cases

Where each approach shines — and why combining them covers the most scenarios.

GPS Best For

  • Field service technicians
  • Delivery and transportation
  • Multi-site consultants
  • Remote workers
  • Construction crews moving between sites

Geofencing Best For

  • Single office or headquarters
  • Construction site with defined perimeter
  • Warehouse or distribution center
  • Retail store location
  • Manufacturing facility

Combined GPS + Geofencing

  • Field workers who must report to a central site
  • Delivery drivers making drops at specific locations
  • Contractors with home offices but central worksite
  • Hybrid remote + office teams

Accuracy Comparison

Understanding the accuracy limitations of each technology helps set realistic expectations and appropriate boundaries.

GPS Accuracy Factors

  • Outdoor clear sky: 3-5 meter accuracy
  • Urban areas with buildings: 5-15 meter accuracy
  • Indoors or covered areas: 50+ meter accuracy or unavailable
  • Weather conditions: Rain, snow can slightly degrade accuracy
  • Device quality: Newer smartphones have better GPS chips

Geofence Radius Recommendations

  • Office buildings: 100-200 meter radius
  • Construction sites: 300-500 meter radius
  • Warehouses: 100-200 meter radius
  • Retail stores: 150-300 meter radius
  • Flexible settings: Allow employer configuration

Implementation Considerations

  1. Employee Consent: Transparently communicate location tracking policies and obtain necessary consent
  2. Privacy Compliance: Ensure GDPR, CCPA, and local law compliance for location data collection
  3. Battery Impact: Inform employees about GPS impact on battery life; offer optimization settings
  4. Network Dependencies: GPS works best with cellular/data connection for transmitting location
  5. Fallback Options: Have alternative verification methods for areas with poor GPS signal
  6. Regular Audits: Periodically review location data accuracy and adjust geofence boundaries as needed
